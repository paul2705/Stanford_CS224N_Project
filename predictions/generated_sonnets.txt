The little love-god, lying once asleep,
Laid by his side his heart-inflaming brand,
Whilst many nymphs that vowed chaste life to keep
Were wont to run the gauntlet of flame.
That night they slept like men
And were but dreamers when new dawned;
But with what fury did their summer rage
Inwardly renew it in maddened wake?
'T was as if fierce fire
Had hastaken a knife from him and wrought
A sharp edge into its sheath
To cut off all conflagration's brine
Within her breast where he lay concealed.
For no matter how hot or cold
He doth lie down on earth nor sleep
Without face upon heaven before
Or looks for relief at any hours

I do believe her, though I know she lies,

Without learning to speak the tongue;
Or else why should not a child

Two loves I have, of comfort and despair,
That like two spirits do suggest me still.
My better angel is a man right fair,
Whose face when he looks on my mind
Doth show his gentleness to see
The inward part clear; yet there
He dost hide what it shows
In the folds whereon seems much matter
To deceive with conceit or folly's purpose:

Than in seeing that which lies concealed
At first hidden before him
And all other parts by reason shown
Or lest they should look upon them false
