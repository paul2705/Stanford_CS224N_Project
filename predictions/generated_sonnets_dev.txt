Thine eyes I love, and they, as pitying me,
Knowing thy heart torment me with disdain,
Have put on black, and loving mourners be,
Thy own widow's blood my main tincture.
But in thee, in thee, in thy most sweet wife,
O, find thy fairest kind of love in this love,
A person not one, but thy most sweet heart doth dwell.
  For what better am I to be thy heir,
  Than this happy shadow whose light hath vanished from thee,
  Which shall lie with my loving heart, thy most sweet heart's prey,
  Thy most blessed love in thee.


1
Beshrew that heart that makes my heart to groan
For that deep wound it gives my friend and me.
Is 't not enough to torture me alone,
Thou art, despite my sin's ease,
Or every time I am my crime and love's virtue,
And that the offense that burns can only be cured by some morn.
So I could not leave my love with me alone,
That men in love might my fate prove
And see their beauty in the cloud, and tell me that they will live.
  I will not make them grow old, and nor will they live old.
  If, by virtue of being in love, I love them all,
  I will never write death.


2
So, now I have confessed that he is thine
And I myself am mortgaged to thy will,
Myself I'll forfeit, so that other mine might live,
And all that wherefore I shall be, with him so far away.
He then made it his fault that he should put on the map
And now my grief hath gone, and it is my fault.
Since then I am by no means alone,
Nor can any one lend me a guess,
Thou by no means for ever am I alone;
To him by his services I still lend him,
  But by him his interest has no place in mine
  And that of his works he can profit no more.


3
Whoever hath her wish, thou hast thy will,
And will to boot, and will in overplus.
More than enough am I that vex thee still,
For I am not far to give back thy love.
When thou art an heir, thou shalt take that trust,
And thou art my heir.
Thy sweetest love, thy most holy child,
A fair maid, thy sweetest youth,
Which goes out to stay till she receives thy happy name.
  Thus I to this do more than want to give back my child.


4
If thy soul check thee that I come so near,
Swear to thy blind soul that I was thy will,
And will, thy soul knows, is admitted there.
Thou art my own posterity, mine own base,
Whose sorrow is a deep wound, and whose proof thou dost look.
And all that wilt I say,
Whilst thou cannot see, where the tears that are wailing,
And all that wilt I say,
That thou art my own light, where thy wailing alone doth stand.
  Or, as the sun should say, all the world doth care.


5
Thou blind fool, Love, what dost thou to mine eyes
That they behold and see not what they see?
They know what beauty is, see where it lies,
And that all beauty is thine alone;
But if thou think thou art there,
Then thou dost, and that truth, such beauty as thou art,
Disappear, being once dead and living again.
For thou art not my sight, but thou art our heart,
Making that part of thy brain thine.
  Therefore I say, Love, thy beauty dost thou be,
  That dost thou change thy state,
  For by thy false course, thou dost break up thy self.

7
O, call not me to justify the wrong
That thy unkindness lays upon my heart;
Wound me not with thine eye but with thy tongue;
I would do thy work, too, for thy imperfections did
Have the blessed gift, but in this way
Thou art a blameless of mine own sin,
And ill deserving of this most crooked recompense;
For if this disgrace of yours were the worth of my life,
Thou wouldst rob me of thy truth, my friend,
And this I am to blame.
  Yet thou wouldst give me up, and I would stand,
  As though in thy ear had I lost thy true word.


8
Be wise as thou art cruel; do not press
My tongue-tied patience with too much disdain,
Lest sorrow lend me words, and words express
The loss of my love, for a small loss was a loss
Such as death should pay, that I should not leave
As in the middle of winter shall appear in my memory,
And, for this reason, turn my thought back.
Then in the end I will leave a lover's bosom;
And to be their friend again may not seem so much
To my lips as in your.
  Such is the melancholy of those who, not knowing that I,
   Even as I am, neglect their beauty.


9
In faith, I do not love thee with mine eyes,
For they in thee a thousand errors note;
But 'tis my heart that loves what they despise,
For this is my love's place.
And then, when thou art love's master,
Thou shalt love nothing but thy beauty,
And do none thy best when thou dost love
Who best be the weakest of all,
For thou art a strong heart, too weak to bear
And who, with thy love, gives thee weakness.
  For with thy love I fear that thy faults be,
  And in me none such faults thou art,
  To him that gives me strength and strength.


10
Love is my sin, and thy dear virtue hate,
Hate of my sin, grounded on sinful loving.
O, but with mine compare thou thine own state,
For thy love in mine eyes is true;
Thy love is so much worse than mine own,
That I love thee in that which I love,
And therefore thou gavest my friend,
And, by thee alone, with mine heart
Which alone bearest to thee makes love,
Who being well loved is oftentimes worse
Against myself, so that I love thee less.
  Thou have I in my esteem, not so much in mine self.


11
Lo, as a careful huswife runs to catch
One of her feathered creatures broke away,
Sets down her babe, and makes all swift dispatch,
Wherea my dear baby's wanderer may
Distribute her garments in all his lands
As well as in our way; but, seeing his strength
In all his growing body, I think the prisoner
Is more in the fear of being ill than of being alive.
  I well remember when she had she thought that she would not live,
  And yet I see that now, having her to blame,
  I can find her thyself, but in a less pleasant place.
